# Visibility and compliance gaps: One Source Medical Center / One Medical Clinic and AVA Injury Clinic (Taylorsville, UT)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method: about 37 WebSearch calls (WebFetch and direct HTTP were blocked, so the work relies on search-result snippets and the search tool's summaries). Labels used: **CONFIRMED** (a source says it directly), **INFERRED** (my reasoning from the sources), **NOT FOUND** (searched for and not located). This is informational research, not legal advice.

**Search-engine caveat (applies to Section 1):** WebSearch is a US web index that returns about 10 organic links per query. It has no Google Map Pack or Local Pack, no location personalization, and no ad slots. These rankings show who holds web/organic authority for each query. They are **not** Google's actual SERP. Google's Map Pack for Taylorsville may look quite different, and the clinic's Google Business Profile is likely its main local asset (INFERRED).

## 4. Section 1557 language access: top 15 non-English languages in Utah for the notice of availability

## 5. Beneficiary inducement: CMP nominal-value limits, the local transportation safe harbor, free Uber rides and "free appointments" for Medicaid patients, and Utah Medicaid NEMT

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: OIG's nominal-value limits are still **$15 per item and $75 in aggregate per beneficiary per year** (OIG policy statement, December 7, 2016). Cash and cash equivalents never qualify, and no later update was found. CONFIRMED: The local transportation safe harbor (42 CFR 1001.952(bb)) protects free rides only if the entity **does not publicly market or advertise** them, among other conditions. AVA's public "free Uber" advertising therefore falls outside the safe harbor for any Medicare or Medicaid patient (INFERRED). CONFIRMED: Utah Medicaid already covers non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT) for Traditional Medicaid members through fee-for-service. Molina does not cover NEMT.

### Cited Findings
- The CMP beneficiary-inducement provision targets remuneration likely to influence a Medicare or Medicaid beneficiary's choice of a particular provider. Per Hall Render (2016), the penalty is up to $10,000 per wrongful act. — [Feldesman](https://training.feldesman.com/node/1218); [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2016/12/07/oig-increases-nominal-value-dollar-limit-cmp-law/)
- The December 7, 2016 OIG Policy Statement raised "nominal value" from $10 per item and $50 a year to **$15 per item and $75 in annual aggregate per beneficiary**. Cash and cash equivalents are excluded. OIG said it would review the limits periodically for inflation. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2016/12/recent-oig-publications-address-aks-safe-harbors-cmp-regulations-and-nominal-value-policy); [Holland & Hart copy of the OIG Policy Statement](https://www.hollandhart.com/pdf/OIG-Policy-Statement-Gifts-of-Nominal-Value.pdf); [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2016/12/07/oig-increases-nominal-value-dollar-limit-cmp-law/)
- Cash equivalents include checks and general-purpose debit cards. Gift cards limited to a store or purpose, such as gas cards, are not cash equivalents but still count toward the limits. — [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/Alerts/2016/12/OIG-Raises-Nominal-Value-Gifts-Medicare-Medicaid-Beneficiaries)
- The $15/$75 figures were still in use in 2024 (Colorado Medicaid OM 24-015). No newer OIG adjustment was found. — [Colorado HCPF OM 24-015](https://hcpf.colorado.gov/sites/hcpf/files/HCPF%20OM%2024-015%20Compliance%20Guidance%20on%20Offering%20Gifts%20and%20Other%20Inducements%20to%20Beneficiaries.pdf)
- Local transportation safe harbor, 42 CFR 1001.952(bb):
  - "Eligible entity" excludes entities that primarily supply health care items (DME suppliers, pharmaceutical companies, pharmacies).
  - The entity must not publicly market or advertise the free transportation. No marketing of health care items may occur during the ride, and drivers may not be paid per beneficiary transported.
  - Rides are for established patients, both of the entity and of the destination provider.
  - The entity must bear the cost.
  - Air, luxury and ambulance-level transport are excluded.
  — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/alert/hhs-oig-issues-new-anti-kickback-safe-harbor-and-civil-monetary-penalty-exceptions); [JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/oig-implements-new-anti-kickback-37882/); [Bricker Graydon chart](https://brickergraydon.com/1178)
- Distance limits: 25 miles in urban areas. The 2020 rule raised the rural limit from 50 to 75 miles and removed the distance limit for patients discharged from inpatient facilities. — [Faegre Drinker (Dec 2020)](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2020/12/oig-revises-local-transportation-safe-harbor)
- Uber Health publishes guidance on safe-harbor-compliant rideshare for providers. — [Uber Health safe harbor article](https://www.uberhealth.com/us/en/resources/articles/safe-harbor-regulations)
- Utah Medicaid NEMT:
  - Coverage: Traditional Medicaid members without regular access to transportation. CHIP, UPP, QMB, SLMB, QI and Emergency Medicaid members are excluded.
  - Booking: call DWS at 1-800-662-9651, at least 3 business days ahead.
  - Options: personal transportation, mass transit (UTA bus, TRAX, paratransit) and special services. Molina's page also lists UTA FlexTrans and LogistiCare (now ModivCare) door-to-door service.
  — [Utah Medicaid NEMT brochure 2022](https://medicaid.utah.gov/Documents/pdfs/NEMT_brochure2022.pdf); [Utah Medicaid Transportation Provider Manual 1-24](https://medicaid.utah.gov/Documents/manuals/pdfs/Medicaid%20Provider%20Manuals/Medical%20Transportation/Transportation1-24.pdf); [Utah NEMT flyer 1.2024](https://medicaid.Utah.gov/Documents/pdfs/NEMT%20flyer1.2024.pdf); [ModivCare Utah](https://www.modivcare.com/facilities/ut)
- Molina Healthcare of Utah: "Non-emergency medical transportation services are not covered by Molina Healthcare. They may be covered by Utah State Fee for Service." — [Molina UT Transportation Services](https://www.molinahealthcare.com/members/ut/hp/medicaid/icare/Coverd/tnsp.aspx)
- In Utah PI care, patients often pay nothing out of pocket because providers bill the auto insurer directly. This is the usual basis for "free appointment" messaging. — [KSL Brandview](https://www.ksl.com/article/51489419/utah-clinic-focuses-exclusively-on-treating-victims-of-accidents)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Free Uber rides fail the safe harbor's "no public marketing or advertising" condition because AVA advertises them on social media. Each ride likely also exceeds $15 in retail value. If a ride goes to a Medicaid or Medicare beneficiary whose visit is billed to Medicaid, Molina or Medicare, it becomes unprotected remuneration and risks liability under both the CMP and the Anti-Kickback Statute. Inducement law does not restrict the same rides for patients whose care is billed only to auto insurance (PIP or third-party liability) with no federal program billing. State insurance-fraud law could still apply.
- INFERRED: The free-ride rationale is weaker for Medicaid patients because Utah Medicaid already offers NEMT (via DWS and ModivCare) to Traditional Medicaid members. The clinic could refer those patients to NEMT instead of paying for rides.
- INFERRED: Free drinks or snacks at the visit are likely within nominal value ($15/$75) if they are not cash and are modest.
- INFERRED: "Tu cita es totalmente GRATIS" is accurate only for PIP-billed accident visits. Aimed at Medicaid patients, an advertised free visit would be a routine cost-sharing waiver or remuneration. Utah Medicaid copays are small, but the waiver must not be advertised. The free message should be scoped explicitly to auto-insurance claims and kept off the One Source/Molina primary-care pages.
- INFERRED (from knowledge; verify): The CMP has other exceptions, such as "promotes access to care and poses a low risk of harm" (42 CFR 1003.110). Advertised rides tied to a high-value PI service are unlikely to fit them.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: The current inflation-adjusted CMP amount per violation under 1320a-7a(a)(5) for 2026. Sources gave only the $10,000 base from 2016. My recollection that the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2018 doubled it to $20,000, plus inflation adjustments, is unverified.
- NOT FOUND: Whether Utah's Adult Expansion Medicaid members receive NEMT. The brochure lists exclusions but the snippet did not address the expansion population. The eCFR text of 1001.952(bb) and OIG's preamble language on rideshare were also not retrieved directly.

## 6. FTC Rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465): incentivized reviews, review suppression, and the 2026 penalty

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: Since **October 21, 2024**, Part 465 bars businesses from offering incentives **conditioned, expressly or implicitly, on a review expressing a particular sentiment**. It also bars review suppression through unjustified legal threats, intimidation or false accusations, and bars presenting filtered reviews as all or most reviews. The maximum civil penalty is **$53,088 per violation**: this is the 2025 adjustment, and the eCFR still showed it in mid-2026. No 2026 adjustment was found.

### Cited Findings
- The final rule was announced in August 2024 and published in the Federal Register on August 22, 2024 (FR Doc. 2024-18519). It took full effect on October 21, 2024. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241023-ftc-ban-fake-review)
- The rule covers six practices: fake or false reviews and testimonials, buying reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression and misrepresentation, and fake social-media indicators. — [Crowell](https://crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/final-rule-announced-the-ftc-strengthens-its-enforcement-capacity-against-deceptive-reviews-and-testimonials); [eCFR 16 CFR 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465)
- Incentives: the rule "does not prohibit incentives for reviews, as long as there is no express or implied requirement that the reviews express a particular sentiment." Implied conditions count; the FTC gives the example of a coupon for telling people how much you loved your visit. Endorsement Guides disclosure is still expected. Liability falls on the business, not the reviewer. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [16 CFR Part 255 Endorsement Guides](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255)
- Suppression (§ 465.7) prohibits:
  - using an unjustified legal threat, a physical threat, intimidation or a false public accusation to prevent or remove a review. An "unjustified legal threat" is a threat of a baseless action, such as defamation over truthful speech or opinion;
  - misrepresenting displayed reviews as all or most reviews when reviews were suppressed by rating or negative sentiment. Sorting by helpfulness is not suppression.
  — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [23 State AGs comment](https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/News-Room/Current-News/2023.9.29%20Comment%20of%2023%20State%20AGs%20(FTC%20Consumer%20Reviews%20and%20Testimonials).pdf)
- Paying to remove truthful negative reviews is not banned by Part 465 itself, but the FTC says it may violate the FTC Act. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- Penalty history: the 2025 adjustment, effective January 17, 2025, raised the Section 5(m)(1)(A) maximum from $51,744 to **$53,088**. 16 CFR 1.98 on eCFR (current through July 13, 2026) and a mirror checked September 21, 2026 still show $53,088. — [FTC 2025 adjustment](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87476); [eCFR 16 CFR 1.98](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-L/section-1.98); [CustomsMobile 1.98](https://www.customsmobile.com/regulations/16/1.98)
- The FTC sent formal warning letters under the rule to nearly a dozen companies in late 2025, requiring written confirmation of corrective steps within five days. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-enforcement-under-consumer-review-rule/pdf/); [FTC 2025 warning letter template](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-Fake-Review-Warning-Template.pdf)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: Each fake or conditioned review can count as a separate violation, so exposure adds up quickly for a clinic with about 223 Birdeye reviews. Any campaign offering patients a gift or free service for a "5-star" review would violate § 465.4. A neutral request, such as "leave an honest review", with any incentive disclosed is allowed under Part 465. Google's own policies separately prohibit incentivized reviews.
- INFERRED: In healthcare, incentives for reviews from Medicaid or Medicare patients also count toward the $15/$75 nominal-value limits in Section 5.
- INFERRED: "Review gating" means sending only satisfied patients to Google and unhappy ones to a private form. If the site then shows only positive reviews as representative, it risks § 465.7(b) misrepresentation.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: Any 2026 FTC inflation adjustment. FTC usually publishes in January, but no 2026 figure surfaced and eCFR still showed $53,088 in July 2026. Check the Federal Register for a January 2026 notice.

## 7. Utah: solicitation of motor-vehicle accident victims by health care providers, and the status of 2023 S.B. 246

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED: **S.B. 246 (2023), "Accident Report Access Amendments", is law.** It was signed March 23, 2023, took effect May 3, 2023 (Laws 2023, ch. 522), and amends Utah Code 41-6a-404. Using accident-report information to market services to a person involved in the crash is a class A misdemeanor. NOT FOUND: Utah has no time-window ban (such as 30 days) on health-care-provider solicitation of crash victims of the kind Kentucky, Ohio and Texas adopted. The cited "Utah Code 31A-31-110" could not be located. Utah instead reaches runners through the Insurance Fraud Act (31A-31-102/-103) and bars paid patient referrals in the Chiropractic Practice Act (58-73-501).

### Cited Findings
- S.B. 246 (2023), "Accident Report Access Amendments", sponsored by Sen. Michael K. McKell and Rep. Nelson T. Abbott, amends § 41-6a-404:
  - It prohibits using an accident report to market services to a person involved in the accident, and prohibits obtaining a report if not authorized by statute.
  - It narrows "agent" to a formally engaged attorney, and separately lists a general acute hospital with an ER that treated the person.
  - The House committee voted 9-0-4 in favor on February 28, 2023. The governor signed it March 23, 2023, effective May 3, 2023, as Laws 2023, ch. 522.
  — [S.B. 246 status page](https://le.utah.gov/~2023/bills/static/SB0246.html); [S.B. 246 enrolled text](https://le.utah.gov/~2023/bills/sbillenr/SB0246.htm); [House committee report](https://le.utah.gov/~2023/comreport/SB246H10.pdf)
- A Senate floor amendment made knowingly obtaining a report without authorization, or knowingly using report information to market services (including legal representation), a class A misdemeanor. — [S.B. 246 floor amendment](https://le.utah.gov/~2023/pamend/sb0246.hcap.01.pdf); [Citizen Portal summary](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/3628589/Utah/Senate-passes-Senate-Bill-246-targeting-misuse-of-police-reports)
- 2025 H.B. 222 further amends § 41-6a-404, expanding the accident-report definition and the release rules. Whether it changed the marketing ban was not determined. — [2025 H.B. 222 enrolled](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/enrolled/HB0222.pdf)
- The Utah Insurance Fraud Act defines a "runner" as a person who procures clients for someone seeking insurance benefits or asserting a claim, including a "capper" or "steerer". 2004 H.B. 171 added a prohibition on employing, using or acting as a runner to engage in fraudulent acts (31A-31-103). — [Utah Code Title 31A Ch. 31](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title31A/Chapter31/C31A-31_1800010118000101.pdf); [2004 H.B. 171 enrolled](https://le.utah.gov/~2004/bills/hbillenr/HB0171.htm); [31A-31-103 (2019 version)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title31A/Chapter31/C31A-31-S103_2019051420190514.pdf)
- The Utah Chiropractic Physician Practice Act, § 58-73-501, lists as unprofessional conduct "(16) sharing professional fees with a person who is not licensed under this chapter; and (17) paying a person for a patient referral." Administrative rule R156-73-501 exists, but its text was not retrieved. — [FindLaw 58-73-501](https://codes.findlaw.com/ut/title-58-occupations-and-professions/ut-code-sect-58-73-501/); [Utah Code Title 58 Ch. 73](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title58/Chapter73/C58-73_1800010118000101.pdf); [LII R156-73](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/commerce/title-R156/rule-R156-73)
- Comparators (not Utah):
  - Kentucky H.B. 153 (2015): bars health-care-provider solicitation of crash victims for 30 days.
  - Ohio R.C. 1349.05: bars practitioner contact for 30 days.
  - Texas H.B. 1519 (2007): bars solicitation for 31 days.
  — [Kentucky HB 153](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/15RS/hb153.html); [Ohio 1349.05](https://codes.ohio.gov/orc/1349.05v1); [Texas HB 1519 analysis](https://lrl.texas.gov/scanned/hroBillAnalyses/80-0/HB1519.PDF)
- A search for "Utah Code 31A-31-110" returned no such section text. — [Justia Utah Code 2025 index](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2025/)

### Inferences
- INFERRED: AVA and One Source may not use police crash reports, or lists derived from them, to send direct mail, texts or calls to crash victims. Doing so is a class A misdemeanor under § 41-6a-404 as amended by S.B. 246. Buying "accident leads" from vendors who source them from crash reports carries the same risk.
- INFERRED: Utah has no time-window solicitation ban, so general social ads, such as free-ride offers on Facebook or TikTok, are not barred by a waiting period. Paying any person (a "promotor/a", tow driver or body-shop staff) per referred patient would breach § 58-73-501(17) for the chiropractor. If tied to insurance claims, it could also qualify as runner activity under the Insurance Fraud Act.

### Gaps
- NOT FOUND: Utah Code 31A-31-110. It may not exist or the citation may be wrong. The current codified text of 31A-31-103 on runners and the full R156-73-501 text were also not retrieved.
- NOT FOUND: Any Utah bill from 2024–2026 creating a crash-victim solicitation waiting period. One extended search found none.
