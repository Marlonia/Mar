## 1. Demographics: Taylorsville, West Valley City and the west Salt Lake Valley (latest Census ACS)

### Takeaway
Taylorsville (about 58k people) is about 26% Hispanic and 26% non-English-at-home, with 13.8% of under-65s uninsured. Next door, West Valley City (about 138k) and Kearns are 42-43% Hispanic. West Valley City has 42.8% non-English-at-home and 19.1% of under-65s uninsured, roughly double the U.S. rate. So the clinic's 3-5 mile catchment holds a large Spanish-speaking population with high uninsured rates, the core audience for a bilingual self-pay, family-medicine and PIP clinic.

### Inferences
- Adding the Hispanic shares of Taylorsville (about 15k), West Valley City (about 59k = 43.3% × 138k), Kearns (about 16k) and West Jordan (about 30k) gives roughly 120k Hispanic residents within a short drive of 5400 S. This is my own arithmetic from the cited figures.
- West Valley City's under-65 uninsured rate (19.1%) is about double the U.S. rate, and Taylorsville's (13.8%) is about 1.4x. That makes a published self-pay price list and No Surprises Act Good Faith Estimates (see Q3) commercially important, not just a compliance item.
- Incomes are near or above the national median ($86-92k). So the market is working-class to middle-income families, often uninsured or on high-deductible plans, not mainly very low-income. That fits a low-priced cash or membership offer, the model Vamos Health uses.

### Gaps

A statewide Latino-children figure (11.1% to 17.3%, 2016-2018, UnidosUS/Georgetown) showed up in a search summary, but I could not tie it to a primary URL, so it is excluded.
- Median household income and uninsured rates for Kearns, Murray, West Jordan and Midvale were not collected.
- Utah DHHS and Salt Lake County health-department data were not reached.

## 2. Competitor benchmark: digital patient experience (8-12+ clinics in or near Taylorsville)

### Takeaway
The large systems nearby (Intermountain, University of Utah Westridge, St. Mark's/MountainStar) offer 7-day or evening hours, walk-in urgent care, published or aggregator self-pay prices, 24/7 telehealth ($79 cash at Intermountain) and Spanish-speaking providers searchable by language. The bilingual, Hispanic-focused competitors (Vamos Health, CHC Oquirrh View) win on price: $85/month membership or a sliding fee. The auto-injury clinics (Dr. Calvin's, Car Injury Clinic, AVA, Utah Accident Clinic, Avista) compete on Spanish websites, free consultations, same-day X-rays, free rides and, for Dr. Calvin's, more than 1,000 Google reviews. One Source is open only about 3 days a week with split shifts, uses a Gmail contact address, has inconsistent branding and has no reviews I could find. It also shares its building with AVA Injury Clinic, a Spanish-first auto-injury clinic.

## 3. Digital table stakes for small primary-care and injury clinics (2025-2026) and patient-expectation data

### Takeaway
Patients clearly want self-scheduling: 80% want to book anytime from home or a phone (Experian 2025). Yet most practices see under 25% of patients actually self-scheduling (MGMA 2025), so offering it is table stakes even though uptake is modest. Reviews drive choice: 84% check reviews before choosing a provider, and about 3 in 4 refuse to book below 4 stars. Texting is the preferred reminder channel. For Hispanic patients, Spanish matters most for immigrants (58% prefer a Spanish-speaking provider), and Spanish speakers lean heavily on social media and WhatsApp. Federal law already requires Good Faith Estimates for self-pay patients.

### Cited Findings
**Online self-scheduling**
- Experian Health "State of Patient Access 2025" (survey of 1,004 U.S. adults and 213 revenue-cycle decision makers, Feb 2025): 80% of patients want to schedule appointments anytime from home or a mobile device. 54% of providers offer self-scheduling and another 10% plan to within 6 months. 37% of providers say getting patients to use digital tools is their biggest challenge, and 55% say patients are unsure how to navigate self-scheduling. 52% of patients want more digital options. — [Experian plc press release](https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2025/-improving-patient-access--experian-health-survey-highlights-pro); [Business Wire, Apr 28, 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250428533529/en); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/better-patient-access-tools-help-healthcare-providers-attract-retain-patients)
- Experian's 2021 survey: 73% of consumers want to schedule their own doctor appointments online. — search summary of [Experian/Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250428533529/en) (2021 figure, older)
- MGMA Stat poll (July 29, 2025, n=244): 71% of practices have fewer than 25% of patients self-scheduling digitally, 21% have 25-50%, 5% have 51-75% and 3% have over 75%. Nov 2024: 73% in the under-25% band. — [MGMA Stat](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/meeting-pressure-on-patient-digital-self-scheduling)
- MGMA Stat (July 22, 2025, n=270): 13% of practices do not offer telehealth and 3% are unsure, implying about 84-87% offer it. — [MGMA Stat](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/bridging-strategic-uncertainty-as-the-telehealth-cliff-is-replaced-by-firm-ground)
- Caution: aggregator sites cite an "MGMA" claim that 71% of patients want to self-schedule telehealth. This was not verifiable on MGMA's pages and may confuse the practice-side 71% figure. — [schedulingkit telehealth stats](https://schedulingkit.com/statistics/telehealth-statistics) (unverified)

**Online reviews and provider choice**
- rater8 survey (over 1,000 U.S. patients, reported Feb 2025): 84% check online reviews before selecting a provider, 51% read at least 6 reviews, and 40% say reviews led them to cancel or reconsider a provider. — [Medical Economics, Feb 18, 2025](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/patients-trust-online-reviews-but-they-don-t-leave-them)
- rater8's later (third annual) report: 3 in 4 patients refuse to book with a provider rated below 4 stars, and 55% have canceled or avoided an appointment because of online reviews. — reported in Medical Economics coverage; URL attribution uncertain: [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/how-ai-online-reviews-and-rising-standards-are-redrawing-the-path-to-care) or [Medical Economics slideshow](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/do-online-reviews-of-doctors-matter-)
- Software Advice (older, over 2,000 patients): 72% use online reviews as the first step in choosing a new physician, and 48% would go out-of-network for better reviews. — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/patient-experience/survey-48-of-patients-would-choose-out-of-network-providers-with-better-reviews/); [HC Innovation Group](https://www.hcinnovationgroup.com/population-health-management/patient-engagement/news/13027701/survey-most-patients-use-healthcare-review-sites-to-choose-docs)
- Press Ganey: 65.4% of consumers use search engines such as Google to find a doctor, and 51.1% go online first when choosing a new PCP, vs 23.8% who seek a provider referral. The date is unclear. — [MDedge](https://ma1.mdedge.com/node?page=1863) (attribution from search summary)
- Vendor-sponsor caveat: rater8 and Software Advice sell reputation and marketing software. — (note)

**Text reminders and messaging**
- Phreesia survey (about 14,000 patients; date not shown): 48% prefer text reminders vs 29% phone and 21% email. Preference for text is about 50% in most age groups and 34% at 65+. — [Phreesia blog](https://www.phreesia.com/blog-appointment-reminders-which-methods-do-patient-prefer/)
- Sinch "Patient communication in healthcare 2026" (1,000 U.S. patients; vendor): 84% are more likely to attend if they get a text reminder, and texting has surpassed email and portals as the preferred channel. — [Sinch](https://sinch.com/engage/resources/business-messaging/patient-communication-in-healthcare-2026/)

**Hispanic-patient-specific evidence**
- Pew Research Center ("5 facts about Hispanic Americans and health care", Oct 30, 2023; 2021 survey): 35% of Hispanic adults prefer a Spanish-speaking provider for routine care (58% of immigrants vs 12% of U.S.-born). 51% say it makes no difference and 13% would rather not. A third prefer a Hispanic doctor (47% of immigrants vs 20% of U.S.-born). Only 9% of U.S. health practitioners and technicians are Hispanic. — [Pew Research](https://pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/10/30/5-facts-about-hispanic-americans-and-health-care/)
- KFF Health Misinformation Tracking Poll pilot (Hispanic adults): about half use social media at least weekly for health information and advice, 69% among Spanish speakers. Spanish-speaking Hispanic adults are much more likely to use WhatsApp than English-primary Hispanic adults. Personal doctors are by far the most trusted messengers. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-information-trust/poll-finding/addressing-misinformation-among-hispanic-adults-snapshot-from-the-kff-health-misinformation-tracking-poll-pilot/)
- JAMA Network Open scoping review (2025), digital health use among U.S. Spanish speakers: they are less aware of patient portals and receive fewer portal invitations. The review recommends Spanish-targeted outreach and clinician encouragement. One included study found Spanish speakers had higher odds of being new MyChart adopters. — [JAMA Netw Open 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834118)
- Mayo Clinic cross-sectional survey (2024, n=212 Spanish-preferred patients): 73.6% are not comfortable reading or writing in English. — [PMC11666482](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666482/)
- JMIR Formative Research (2025), bilingual community clinic survey: Spanish speakers report language as a significant barrier to portal use. — [JMIR Formative 2025;e66717](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e66717)
- In a safety-net sample, 59% of Spanish speakers had never used a portal vs 42% of English speakers. — search summary; likely [J Gen Intern Med 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-025-09359-z) (attribution uncertain)
- Community-health-clinic telehealth data (2019-2023): among adults 65+, more Spanish-preferring Latino patients used telehealth than English-preferring Latino or non-Hispanic White patients. — [PMC12374211](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12374211/)

**Price transparency for self-pay patients (federal requirement)**
- Under the No Surprises Act, all licensed providers and facilities must give uninsured or self-pay patients a Good Faith Estimate (GFE). No specialty or site of service is exempt. Patients who choose not to use insurance count as self-pay. — [CMS GFE guidance (PDF)](https://CMS.gov/cciio/resources/regulations-and-guidance/downloads/good-faith-estimate-patient-provider-dispute-resolution-process-for-uninsured-or-self-pay-individuals.pdf); [CMA](https://www.cmadocs.org/newsroom/news/view/ArticleId/49641/New-federal-law-requires-ALL-physicians-to-provide-good-faith-estimates-to-uninsured-and-self-pay-patients)
- Timing: within 1 business day if the visit is scheduled 3-10 days out, within 3 business days if scheduled more than 10 days out, and on request. Providers must post a notice that GFEs are available and tell uninsured patients verbally. — [Spencer Fane](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/no-surprises-act-good-faith-estimate-for-self-pay-patients/); [CMS sample GFE](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-sample-good-faith-estimate.pdf); [APA](https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/practice-management/no-surprises-act-implementation)

**Local benchmarks for what "standard" looks like (from Q2)**
- Published or aggregator cash prices (InstaCare $170 base on Solv), membership pricing (Vamos $84.99/month), 24/7 telehealth at $79 (Intermountain Connect Care), 7-day evening urgent care (Westridge), and a Spanish-language location page (Vamos). — see Q2 citations: [Solv](https://www.solvhealth.com/company/instacare); [Herald Extra](https://www.heraldextra.com/news/2026/mar/18/a-better-way-ribbon-cutting-held-at-vamos-health-in-orem/); [Intermountain blog](https://intermountainhealthcare.org/blogs/expanding-virtual-visits-with-connect-care-now-available-in-colorado-and-montana); [U of U urgent care](https://healthcare.utah.edu/primary-care/urgent); [Vamos ES page](https://www.vamoshealth.com/westvalleycityutahespan)

### Inferences
- A defensible 2026 "table stakes" list for a small bilingual clinic, inferred from the data above:
  1. Online self-scheduling, in Spanish too, on mobile, ideally same-day.
  2. Text and WhatsApp reminders and two-way messaging.
  3. Digital intake forms in Spanish.
  4. A Google Business Profile with a steady flow of reviews and a rating of 4+ stars.
  5. A posted self-pay price list plus a GFE notice, legally required.
  6. Some telehealth option.
  7. Full Spanish pages that match the English ones, not machine translation.
  8. Evening or Saturday hours.
- Because portal adoption is low among Spanish speakers and they lean on WhatsApp and social media (KFF), WhatsApp or SMS is a better primary channel for this market than a patient portal.
- Low self-scheduling uptake (MGMA) means online booking supplements phone and WhatsApp booking rather than replacing it. Staff still need to answer calls and messages in Spanish quickly.

## 4. Utah auto-accident context: PIP, how injury clinics market around it, and lead sources

### Takeaway
Utah is a no-fault state. Every auto policy carries Personal Injury Protection (PIP) with at least $3,000 of medical benefits per person. The patient can choose any licensed provider, including chiropractors and PTs, and payment is capped at a state Relative Value Study fee schedule (Rule R590-267). Medical bills over $3,000 open a tort claim against the at-fault driver, which is why injury clinics and personal-injury attorneys are tightly linked. Local injury clinics market "free evaluation", "100% covered", direct PIP billing, same-day X-rays, transportation and Spanish service. Google Ads for PI terms are expensive because attorneys bid on the same searches.

### Cited Findings
**Statute and rules**
- Utah Code §31A-22-307: PIP must cover not less than $3,000 per person for reasonable medical, surgical, hospital, rehabilitation and related expenses. Lost wages are paid at the lesser of $250/week or 85% of income, up to 52 weeks. (PDF version effective 5/3/2023; later amendments not checked.) — [Utah Legislature §31A-22-307 PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title31a/Chapter22/C31A-22-S307_2023050320230503.pdf)
- The $3,000 is a floor per injured person, and higher limits are available (law-firm commentary). — [BAM Injury Law, PIP requirements](https://baminjurylaw.com/blog/utah-pip-coverage-requirements/); [BAM, PIP guide](https://baminjurylaw.com/blog/personal-injury-protection-insurance-utah/)
- Tort threshold (§31A-22-309): an injured person can sue the at-fault driver if medical expenses exceed $3,000 (billed charges) or if the injury falls in a serious-injury category (death, dismemberment, permanent impairment or disfigurement, fracture; lists vary by source). — [BAM Injury Law, serious injury threshold](https://baminjurylaw.com/blog/utah-serious-injury-threshold-lawsuit/) (Spanish version: [BAM /es/](https://baminjurylaw.com/es/blog/utah-serious-injury-threshold-lawsuit/))
- Patients may choose their own provider under PIP. Covered providers include MDs, DOs, chiropractors, physical therapists and dentists. Some policies limit acupuncture and massage. — [BAM, choose doctor under PIP](https://baminjurylaw.com/blog/choose-doctor-pip-insurance-utah/) (law-firm source)
- PIP fee schedule: the Utah Insurance Department has published a Relative Value Study since 1990 to set the "reasonable value" of PIP medical expenses under §31A-22-307(2). The rule is Utah Admin. Code R590-267, with conversion factors updated every two years. Chiropractic conversion factors were added for services on or after Jan 1, 2020. A 2025 amendment notice references the RVP 2025 and RVD 2025 publications for medical, dental and chiropractic offices. — [Utah Insurance Dept, PIP & RVS](https://insurance.utah.gov/consumers/rvs/); [R590-267 amendment Oct 22, 2025 (PDF)](https://insurance.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/267Amend10-22-2025.pdf); [Cornell LII R590-267](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/insurance/title-R590/rule-R590-267); [DAR File 44178 (2019)](https://rules.utah.gov/publicat/bulletin/2019/20191115/44178.htm)

### Inferences
- The $3,000 PIP floor plus the $3,000 tort threshold sets up the typical injury-clinic model: treat within PIP, document thoroughly, and, once bills pass $3,000, coordinate with the patient's attorney (letters of protection, records, narrative reports). One Source's "personal injury evaluation and care program" fits this model, but its public messaging (from snippets) does not explain PIP, "no out-of-pocket" or attorney coordination in Spanish the way competitors do.
- Typical lead sources visible locally: Google Search and Maps (reviews matter heavily), Google Ads (expensive for PI terms), attorney referral relationships, Spanish-language Facebook and Instagram, the Hispanic Chamber, and sponsored local media (KSL Brandview). Spanish radio is likely but unconfirmed (see Gaps).
- Compliance caveat, from background knowledge, not sourced here: paying attorneys or "runners" for patient referrals, or waiving PIP-related cost-sharing, raises anti-kickback and insurance-fraud risks under state law. Any attorney-referral program needs Utah legal review.
- Because the RVS caps PIP payments, the clinic's PIP revenue per visit is set by the state. Growth comes from patient volume and documentation quality, not price.
